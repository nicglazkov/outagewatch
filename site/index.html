The promise

Only four things ever notify you

  • Start An outage started at your location By default a saved address alerts only when an outage actually covers it, not merely nearby. Outage
  • Estimate The restoration estimate moved Only when it shifts by more than 30 minutes. Update
  • Restore Power was restored Sent once the outage at your place is over. Restored
  • PSPS A safety shutoff is planned for your area The one alert that comes through during quiet hours. Planned shutoff

Quiet hours are respected for everything except PSPS warnings. Nothing else, ever.

How it works

One feed, checked every five minutes

Watches PG&E's public outage map for the places you save, and tells you the moment one reaches your address.

PG&E outage feed ---- polled every 5 minutes ----> diff against last snapshot
                                                        |
                     outage covers a place you saved ---+---> push to your phone

Under the hood

Built to be trusted

Plain-language answers

Every outage gets a short, calm "what is going on and when is it coming back", written from the real feed data only. The model may not invent causes or restoration times.

No account, no tracking

No sign-up, no name, no email. Your saved places and an anonymous push token exist only to deliver your alerts. No ads, no analytics.

Open source

The whole thing is MIT-licensed on GitHub: the apps, the backend, and this page. Read exactly what it does with your data.

Honest by design

PG&E's feed gives outages, not grid topology, so OutageWatch never draws circuits it cannot see and never claims more precision than the data has.

Not affiliated with PG&E. Data comes from PG&E's public outage map and can lag behind reality. Do not rely on OutageWatch for safety-critical decisions. For emergencies call 911. Report downed power lines to PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.
